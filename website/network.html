Who finds the blocks

In the first weeks every block was found solo. Today nearly all blocks come from two pools, and most of them from one.

This matters. A pool's operator chooses the transactions and the parent of every block the pool works on, and whoever directs most of the hash power can build the chain with the most work. More pools and more solo miners make the chain harder to steer.

Where the numbers come from

Not every figure on this page has the same weight.

From the chain
Height, difficulty, supply and every chart on this page. The hashrate is the work in the latest blocks divided by the time they took. Anyone who runs a full node can recompute all of it.
Reported
Active miners, sessions, nodes and the hashrate a pool states about itself. These are self-reports. They show a direction, and nobody can check them.
The mirror
This page and the explorer read a database copy of the chain, so that they load quickly. The mirror checks every block it stores, but it is a convenience. The chain is what the full nodes agree on.
Seed nodes
A new node first contacts pool.yskar.app:8646 or yskar-seed2.dynv6.net:8646. Both are run by the same operator. After that, nodes exchange the addresses of other nodes among themselves.
