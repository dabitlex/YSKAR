What is YSKAR

A currency
with a chain of its own.

YSR is the coin of the YSKAR blockchain. The network runs on its own blocks, its own nodes and its own consensus rules, and it is a community project: no company stands behind it.

Its own blockchain

YSKAR does not run on top of another network. It has its own blocks, its own peer-to-peer network and its own explorer.

A fixed supply

The maximum number of YSR is written into the consensus rules. No one can raise it, and new coins become rarer over time.

A fair launch

Nothing was pre-mined or allocated. New YSR come into existence in one way only: as the reward for a block.

Proof of work

Blocks are secured by real computing work. Phones, PCs and graphics cards can take part in mining.

Self-custody

Your wallet lives on your own device. Nobody else holds your keys, including the people who build YSKAR.

Open source

Node, apps, miner and this website are public on GitHub. Anyone can read the code, run it and propose changes.

Anatomy of a block header

Every block starts with exactly 136 bytes. Its hash is double SHA-256 over these bytes, and it must not exceed the target that the difficulty sets.

ver4 height4 prevHash32 merkleRoot32 stateRoot32 time8 diff4 txs4 extra8 nonce8
prevHashThe hash of the previous block. It links the chain: changing an old block would change every hash after it.
merkleRootOne hash over all transactions in the block. A single changed byte in any transfer changes it.
stateRootOne hash over all accounts (balance and nonce) after this block. Every node computes it itself and compares.
nonceThe number miners vary. The first 128 bytes stay the same for a job, so each try needs only two SHA-256 compression steps.

Anatomy of a transfer

A transfer moves YSR from one account to another. It is 168 bytes plus an optional memo.

meta8 from20 to20 amt8 fee8 nonce8 key32 signature64
  • The signature also covers the chain ID, so a transfer signed for YSKAR is invalid on any other network.
  • The nonce counts the transfers an account has sent. The same transfer can never be applied twice.
  • An optional expiry height lets a transfer lapse if it has not been included by then.

EXAMPLE
01 · Mine

Devices compete for the next block.

Phones, PCs and graphics cards compute double SHA-256 hashes. Whoever finds a hash below the network’s target creates the next block.

  • The block pays the block reward plus all fees, to the finder alone or shared in a pool
  • A pool pays every miner directly from the block, by the work contributed
  • The difficulty follows the computing power of the whole network

V6
In development · activation height not yet set Consensus revision 6

The next chapter
of YSKAR.

V6 is a hard fork that changes the account record, the address format and the transaction format. It gives accounts new abilities, raises how many transactions every block can hold and lays the foundation for features that later revisions can build on. It activates at a fixed block height, announced well in advance.

2 of 3

Multi-signature accounts

Accounts that need several signatures to spend, with up to 16 keys. For shared funds, teams and safer savings.

THRESH(2, [A, B, C])
Locked

Time-locked savings

YSR that can only be spent from a chosen block height on. Set aside today, spendable later.

THRESH(2, [A, AFTER T])
Swap

Hash-locked swaps

A payment is released only against a secret, with a refund to the sender after a set height. Two parties can trade without trusting each other.

HASH h · AFTER T
1,000 recipients

Batch payments

One transaction pays up to 1,000 recipients at once. Useful for payouts, salaries and anyone who pays many people.

5,000 per block

2.5× more transactions

Each block can hold up to 5,000 transactions instead of 2,000 today.

255 recipients

Bigger pools

A pool’s block can pay up to 255 recipients directly (254 miners plus the operator’s fee), up from 64 today. Still without the pool holding anyone’s coins.

What stays the same

  • 21 million YSR and the halving schedule
  • 10-minute blocks, SHA-256d and the difficulty rules
  • Every address, key and balance stays valid

What you will need to do

  • Update your wallet and your node before the activation height
  • Miners, the GPU miner and the HiveOS package need no update
  • Receiving YSR and your balance are not affected; an outdated wallet only cannot send

Under the hood

  • A state tree that scales to many thousands of accounts
  • Versioned addresses and a fork ID in every signature
  • Room reserved in every account for a later contract revision

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost anything to take part?

No. The app, Mini App, Node Core and miner are free. You pay for the electricity your device uses; sending YSR costs a small network fee, and a pool keeps its fee (the public pool: 1 %).

What if I lose my phone?

With your 12 words you can restore your wallet on any device. Without them, nobody can recover the funds, not even us.

What is V6, and do I have to do anything?

V6 is the next revision of the consensus rules, a hard fork. It activates at a block height that will be announced well in advance. Your address, keys and balance stay valid. Before that height, update your wallet or app, Node Core and your node; the command-line miner, the GPU miner and the HiveOS package need no update.

Why doesn’t the Play Store app mine?

Google Play does not allow apps that mine cryptocurrency on the device itself. That is why the Android app with mining is available directly on this website.

Is the code open source?

Yes. The entire code – node, app, miner and this website – is public on GitHub and released under the MIT License.

How do I know the numbers are real?

The explorer checks the proof of work of every block in your browser and follows the chain. A full node or the observer goes further and verifies every transfer, signature and balance from block 0 – anyone can run one.
